04Method and verification

The screen sums recorded cash dividends announced in the trailing period, converts percentage-of-par declarations to cash per share using the recorded face value, divides that amount by the dated market price, and sorts descending. That mixes a historical numerator with a point-in-time denominator, so it is a screen rather than a forecast.

  • Verify the numerator: open the issuer's PSX payout history and confirm each cash event, announcement date, financial period and whether it is final, interim or special.
  • Verify face value: a declared percentage is applied to par value, not market price. A wrong face-value assumption produces a wrong cash dividend.
  • Verify the denominator: use the same dated close shown by PSX, then account for splits, bonus issues or other corporate actions affecting comparability.
  • Add missing dimensions: payout coverage, operating cash flow, leverage, liquidity, concentration, tax and subsequent company filings are outside this one-field sort.

Interpretation limit: the first row is not called “best.” A large trailing yield may reflect a special payout, a falling price, stale data, a corporate action or a conversion error. Reproduce the row from PSX issuer records before using it in further research.

06Frequently asked questions

What does the first row mean?
Only that it has the largest stored trailing cash dividend divided by stored share price among observations passing the screen. It is not a quality score, forecast or recommendation.
Why can a trailing dividend yield be misleading?
The numerator can include special or non-recurring cash payouts, while the denominator can fall sharply. The result says nothing by itself about future distributions or total return.
How is the screen reproduced?
Sum cash dividends announced in the trailing period after converting declared percentages using the recorded face value, divide by the dated market price, and sort descending. Verify face value, announcements and price against each issuer's PSX records.
Is this page live?
No. The page displays its stored price date and payout-announcement cutoff. It remains a dated snapshot until both source partitions are collected and the page is rebuilt.
