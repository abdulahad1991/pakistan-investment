What this site is

Pakistan savings and market data, with source dates

pakinvestlysis.com is a free education site for Pakistani savers comparing National Savings certificates, mutual funds, PSX stocks and gold. An automated pipeline attempts market-data refreshes during each trading day; event and monthly series update only when their publishers release a new value. The interface shows source dates and flags stale source groups when a refresh fails. The code is public, and the methodology page documents primary sources and labelled fallbacks.

The site is written and maintained by Abdul Ahad, a software engineer, not a licensed investment adviser. Nothing here is personalised financial advice or a recommendation to buy anything; use the source dates and original documents before relying on a figure.

FAQ

Pakistan investing questions

How much money do I need to start investing in Pakistan?
Minimum amounts differ by product and provider and can change. Check the current official product page, then compare fees, access and loss risk rather than choosing solely on the minimum.
Are National Savings better than mutual funds?
They solve different needs. National Savings products have stated profit terms and eligibility rules; mutual funds hold market assets, charge fees and can gain or lose value. Compare access, risk and time horizon rather than relying on one return figure.
Does the comparison include Islamic products?
The data includes funds marked Shariah-compliant by their providers. Verify the current fund documents and Shariah board disclosures before relying on that classification.
Is PSX investing risky?
Yes. Listed shares can fall sharply, remain below their purchase price and stop paying dividends. This site cannot assess whether that risk is suitable for a particular reader.
Can overseas Pakistanis invest?
The Roshan Digital Account supports remote access to Naya Pakistan Certificates, PSX stocks and funds. Read the overseas investor guide and verify eligibility with SBP.
How often are rates updated?
Collection schedules differ by source, and a failed fetch can leave the last successful value visible. Check the displayed cutoff and data-health warning, then verify the original publisher.
